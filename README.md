# Google AI Ultra 5x: What the $100 Ultra Tier Gets You, Who Should Skip It, and How to Pay for It Outside the US

Google now has two plans called "Ultra," and the cheaper one is the one most people actually want. The confusion is understandable: until May 2026, "AI Ultra" meant a single $250/month subscription. Now it's a family of two.

Here's the short version: **Google AI Ultra 5x is the $100/month Ultra tier** Google launched at I/O 2026, and the "5x" in the name refers to usage limits, not price, not storage, not the number of accounts.

Everything below is the detail that matters — what's inside the plan, how it compares to the $200 sibling, and the part nobody mentions in the launch coverage: actually paying for it if your card, your country, or your Google Account gets in the way.

## The May 2026 restructure, in one paragraph

At I/O 2026, Google did three things at once. It added a new Ultra tier at $99.99/month aimed at developers, technical leads, knowledge workers, and advanced creators. It cut the old top-tier Ultra from $250 to $200/month without removing features. And it renamed both, so the support documentation now presents them as **Google AI Ultra 5x (20 TB)** and **Google AI Ultra 20x (30 TB)** [1][2].

That renaming is why people are searching for "Google AI Ultra 5x" rather than "Google AI Ultra." The plan name is new; the product line isn't.

One detail worth knowing before you budget: the 5x tier is monthly billing only. There's no annual option on it — the Japanese price table published after launch lists it strictly as a monthly charge, while AI Plus and AI Pro still offer yearly plans at a discount [3].

## What you actually get for $99.99/month

The headline benefit is throughput. Ultra 5x gives you **five times the usage limits of AI Pro** in both the Gemini app and Google Antigravity, Google's agent-first development platform [2][4]. Priority access to Antigravity is included too, which matters more than it sounds like if you're queueing behind Pro users on agent runs.

Then there's the storage: **20 TB**, up from whatever your current plan provides, aimed at people with large datasets, codebases, or media libraries [4].

The rest of the spec sheet:

- **Gemini 3.5 Flash** — the new default model for coding and complex agentic work, rolling out across all paid tiers, not just Ultra [2]
- **Deep Think** access (Ultra tiers only — Pro doesn't get it) [3]
- **Nano Banana Pro** image model access [3]
- **A 1M-token context window** (shared with Pro) [3]
- **10,000 Google Flow credits per month**, versus 1,000 on Pro and 200 on Plus [3]
- **4K image and video upscaling** — also Ultra-only [3]
- **Gemini Spark**, Google's 24/7 agent that acts across your Google products, in a US-only beta [2][5]
- **A YouTube Premium individual plan** [2]

On that last point, you'll see conflicting summaries. Google's own announcement says YouTube Premium individual plan [2]; several outlets wrote "YouTube Premium Lite" instead [5][6]. The Lite confusion comes from a separate change — AI Pro subscribers in select countries are getting Premium Lite (valued at $8.99/month) thrown in. For Ultra 5x, the official wording is the full individual plan.

Also included: Health Premium and Home Premium at no extra cost, AI Inbox in Gmail (now expanded from Ultra down to Plus and Pro in the US), and the new Daily Brief digest in the Gemini app [2]. Google Pics and extra voice features in Gmail, Docs, and Keep are slated for Pro and Ultra subscribers later in the summer [2].

## The bigger change isn't the price — it's how limits work

Google used to cap you with daily prompt counts. That's gone. Paid plans now run on a **compute-used model**: your allowance is spent based on how complex your prompt is, which features you're using, and how long the conversation runs [2][4].

Practically:

1. A short text question eats far less of your allowance than a video generation or a long agentic coding run.
2. Your limit refreshes **every five hours**, until you hit a weekly cap.
3. If you hit the cap on the biggest models, Google shifts you down to smaller, faster models rather than cutting you off.
4. Pro and Ultra subscribers can buy pay-as-you-go AI credits for Google Antigravity, Google Flow, and (soon) the Gemini app [1].

For heavy text users — developers, writers, researchers sending hundreds of prompts a day — the compute model is generally more generous than fixed daily caps. For people blending video generation and long agent runs into the same account, it's less predictable. That's a structural trade, not a marketing point, and it's the thing to watch in your first billing cycle.

## Is the $100 tier the right one, or do you need the $200?

Twenty times Pro versus five times Pro is a 4x gap between the two Ultra plans, and it costs an extra $100/month.

The honest read: **if your work is text, code, and research, Ultra 5x is probably the one.** You get Deep Think, Antigravity priority, the 1M context window, and 20 TB, none of which the 20x tier provides exclusively. The difference shows up in sustained, heavy usage — long agentic sessions in Antigravity, or a workflow where 10,000 Flow credits per month isn't close to enough.

The 20x tier earns its price in three specific situations: you're burning through Flow credits for video work at scale, you're running Antigravity agents continuously, or you specifically want **Project Genie**, the world-building prototype that's now global on the 20x plan and includes a Street View-powered feature for creating environments anchored to real locations [2][6].

ZDNET's comparison reached roughly the same conclusion — start with the $100 variant, and move up only if you're actually hitting its ceiling [6]. That's cheap advice to follow because the tiers are monthly, with no annual lock-in.

## Quick answers

**Can I pay annually for AI Ultra 5x?** No — it's monthly billing only. Annual discounts apply to AI Plus and AI Pro [3].

**Does AI Ultra 5x include Project Genie?** No. Project Genie is on the $200 Ultra 20x tier, globally, for users 18 and older [2].

**What happens when I hit my limit?** You drop to smaller, faster models rather than getting cut off, and your allowance refreshes every five hours up to a weekly cap. You can also buy top-up AI credits on Pro and Ultra [1][2].

**Is the storage really 20 TB?** Yes on Ultra 5x. Ultra 20x gets 30 TB [1][3].

**Will family members get access?** Selected AI benefits can be shared with your family group; Google Developer Program premium cannot [1][7].

The $100 tier exists because $250 was a hard sell and the middle of the market was empty — everyone either tolerated Pro's limits or paid nearly nothing. Five times Pro's throughput plus 20 TB for a fifth of the old top-tier price is the actual offer here. Whether it's worth it for you comes down to a single question you can answer better than any launch post: how often did you hit your Pro limits last month? If the answer is "constantly," the $99.99 is easy math. If you're not sure, you're probably still fine on Pro — and that's not a failure, that's just not being the customer this tier was built for.
